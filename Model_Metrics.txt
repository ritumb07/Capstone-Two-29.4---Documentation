1. MODEL SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Candidates compared (5-fold Stratified Cross-Validation, scored on ROC-AUC,
on the training split only):

    Model                   Mean CV ROC-AUC     Std Dev
    ---------------------   ----------------    -------
    Logistic Regression          0.7220          0.0166      <-- SELECTED
    XGBoost                      0.6909          0.0128
    Random Forest                0.6789          0.0168

Selection rule: highest mean cross-validated ROC-AUC.
Winner: LOGISTIC REGRESSION (regularized linear model).

Why a linear model won here: churn risk in this dataset is driven by a
largely additive combination of factors (contract type, tenure, pricing,
support add-ons) without strong non-linear interactions, so a well-
regularized linear model matches (and here, slightly exceeds) tree
ensembles — while remaining far easier to audit and explain to
non-technical stakeholders.

2. FINAL MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm:            Logistic Regression (scikit-learn LogisticRegression)
Class balancing:      class_weight = "balanced"
Selection method:     RandomizedSearchCV (n_iter=30, 5-fold Stratified CV,
                       scoring = roc_auc, random_state = 42)

Final (tuned) hyperparameters:
    C          = 0.03        (inverse regularization strength; smaller = more regularization)
    penalty    = l2
    solver     = liblinear
    max_iter   = 2000
    class_weight = balanced

Best cross-validated ROC-AUC during search: 0.7223
Model intercept (log-odds space):           -0.1337

Preprocessing pipeline: sklearn ColumnTransformer
    - numeric  -> StandardScaler
    - categorical -> OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore", drop="if_binary")
Fit only on the training split (no leakage into the test set).

6. HOW TO READ THESE NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ROC-AUC of 0.73 means the model ranks a randomly chosen churner above a
  randomly chosen non-churner about 73% of the time — a solid, well
  above-chance (0.50) signal for a single-snapshot behavioral dataset.
- Recall of 0.66 on the churn class means the model correctly flags
  about 2 out of every 3 customers who actually go on to churn — this
  is the number that matters most for a proactive retention campaign,
  since a missed churner is a lost customer.
- Precision of 0.36 means roughly 1 in 3 customers flagged as high-risk
  actually churns. This is an expected and acceptable trade-off given
  class_weight="balanced" was used to prioritize recall: retention
  outreach (an email, a call, a discount offer) is inexpensive relative
  to losing a paying customer, so casting a somewhat wider net is the
  right business trade-off here. The classification THRESHOLD (default
  0.50 above) can be raised to trade some recall for higher precision if
  the retention team's campaign capacity is limited — see the
  precision-recall curve in the notebook (Section 6) to pick a threshold
  that matches actual campaign budget/capacity.
